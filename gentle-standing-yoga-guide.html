Gentle Standing Yoga for Seniors: A Safe Start

Updated October 2026 · 5 min read · By the Serenity Yoga Team

Once you feel comfortable with seated movement, gentle standing yoga is the next wonderful step. Using a chair for support, these slow standing sequences build the leg strength and balance that keep you independent and confident in daily life.

Why standing practice matters after 60

  • Stronger legs and hips — making stairs, walks, and standing up easier
  • Better balance — one of the best defenses against falls
  • Improved posture — reducing neck and back strain
  • More energy — standing circulation boosts alertness

6 supported standing poses

  1. Supported Mountain (1 min): Stand behind your chair, hands resting on the back. Feet hip-width, breathe deeply, and feel tall through the crown of your head.
  2. Rising Arms (30 sec): Keep one hand on the chair and sweep the other arm up, then switch. Lift only as far as comfortable.
  3. Supported Side Stretch (30 sec each side): Hand on the chair, reach the free arm overhead and lean gently away.
  4. Heel Lifts (10 reps): Hold the chair and slowly rise onto the balls of your feet, then lower with control. Builds calf strength and balance.
  5. Supported Leg Swing (10 each leg): Facing the chair, gently swing one leg forward and back, then side to side.
  6. Standing Backbend (20 sec): Hands on your lower back, gently lift the chest toward the ceiling. Skip this one if you have spinal concerns.

Safety tips for standing practice

  • Place your chair on a rug or non-slip floor, and stand facing it or beside it — never behind.
  • Keep a wall within reach for extra security.
  • Wear supportive, non-slip shoes or practice barefoot on a grippy surface.
  • Never hold your breath — move with slow, steady breathing.
